Subscriber.dev doesn't send email itself. It hands each email to a provider you connect, such as Resend, Amazon SES or Postmark. You keep your own sending reputation and pay the provider directly.

Each environment needs at least one provider. Without one, email steps fail with "No email integration configured".

## Connect a provider

| Provider | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| [SMTP](/integrations/smtp) | Any SMTP provider. |
| [Cloudflare Email](/integrations/cloudflare) | Domains on Cloudflare. Bounces are picked up automatically. |

You can connect more than one, and the next is tried if one fails. See [Integrations](/integrations/overview#more-than-one-email-provider).

## Avoid the spam folder

Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook check that email really comes from you. With your provider:

1. Verify your sending domain.
2. Add the SPF and DKIM DNS records the provider gives you.
3. Add a DMARC record, such as `v=DMARC1; p=none;` to start with.

Subscriber.dev already does the rest: every email has a plain-text version, an unsubscribe link, and one-click unsubscribe headers, which Gmail and Yahoo require from bulk senders.

Sending from a subdomain, such as `mail.acme.com`, keeps your marketing email's reputation separate from your main domain.

## Bounces

A bounce is an email that couldn't be delivered. A hard bounce means the address doesn't exist; a soft bounce is temporary, such as a full inbox.

When a hard bounce comes back, the subscriber is [opted out](/subscribers#opting-in-and-out) so you don't keep sending to a dead address, which hurts your reputation. Soft bounces are recorded but don't opt anyone out.

Bounces are picked up automatically from [Cloudflare Email](/integrations/cloudflare#bounces). They aren't picked up from [SMTP](/integrations/smtp#bounces) providers yet.

[Analytics](/cli#other-commands) shows sends and bounces for an environment.
